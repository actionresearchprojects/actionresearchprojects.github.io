    Zone 1 — mechanically cooled and ventilated¶

    Zone 1 spaces run air conditioning efficiently and, in less harsh climates, can switch off and work naturally ventilated. Air conditioning without adequate room ventilation is strongly discouraged.

    Zone 1 areas must be able to operate energy-efficiently in mechanical cooling and ventilation mode — and, for less harsh climates, to be able to turn off the mechanical systems and operate effectively in natural ventilation mode when external conditions allow.

    Balancing cooling and dehumidification¶

    Dehumidification — 'latent cooling of the air' — should be optimised alongside air cooling — 'sensible cooling of the air' — if suitable equipment and controls are available.

    Why this matters is explained in humidity and thermal comfort.

    Ventilating a room that is being air conditioned¶

    Ventilation of rooms, for when air conditioning is running, may be:

    • a continuous mechanical extract ventilation (MEV) fan — either for individual rooms, or a larger extract fan serving several rooms — with fresh air supply to each room via trickle vents; or
    • a mechanical ventilation system supplying fresh air and extracting stale air from rooms with 'coolth' recovery (MVCR), if budget and supply chain allow.

    Air conditioning without ventilation

    Air conditioning without adequate room ventilation is strongly discouraged.

    ARC has set out what it would like from better-suited equipment in an R&D brief for hot humid climates.

    Relationship to the other zones¶

    The additional measures needed for a Zone 1 space sit on top of those already required for Zone 2 and Zone 3 spaces. The purpose is to ensure that energy use, carbon emissions and running costs are minimised.

    A Zone 1 space still has to work when the power is off. See designing a heat refuge for the passive measures that make that possible.
